Dissertation

Hallucination Span Detection in Large Language Models

MSc dissertation, Department of Computer Science, University of Sheffield · 2025

  • Benchmarked open-source LLMs against proprietary state-of-the-art models on hallucination span detection, finding them comparable in detection and subject to the same limitations.
  • Showed open-source models are highly effective context retrievers, with end-to-end pipeline performance dropping by only 1–2% when proprietary retrieval engines were replaced.
  • Explored prompt modification and temperature-varied ensembling of the best-performing model.

Research positions

Formal v/s Contextual Cues for Grammatical Gender Prediction

Visiting Student Researcher, Centre for Language Evolution, University of Edinburgh · August 2021 – May 2022

  • Trained vector representations from the contextual and formal properties of words and compared their usability in a noun gender prediction task.
  • Used gender-debiasing methods to isolate the gender information in a word vector, showing that the human reliance on semantic over phonological cues shows up in language models too.

Diachronic Distributed Word Representations as Models of Mental Lexicon

Student Researcher, Cognitive Neuroscience Lab, BITS Pilani Goa Campus · June 2020 – May 2021

  • Used a temporally sliced corpus to learn distributed word representations of child speech and child-directed speech under a curriculum-learning setting.
  • Demonstrated the ease of usage and effectiveness of diachronic distributed word representations in modelling lexical development, presented at the ACL 2022 Student Research Workshop.
